# Content Forensics Framework: Algorithmic Mimicry & EU AI Act Compliance

**Open-Source Computational & Regulatory Blueprint for Doctoral Research:**  
*Algorithmic Mimicry and the Crisis of the Gatekeeper: AI Humanization and the Inflation of the Public Sphere*

## Project Overview
This repository houses the operational methodology and scripts for a doctoral research project bridging critical media studies, digital humanities, and technology governance. 

As commercial AI humanizers are increasingly deployed to bypass statistical classifiers, newsroom gatekeepers face a systemic crisis: the structural flattening of the linguistic commons. Rather than building black-box detection tools, this framework applies Python-based corpus linguistics and institutional ethnography to operationalize **"Active Editorial Control"** under **Article 50 of the EU AI Act**.

## The Five-Stage Taxonomy of Content Provenance
To replace binary "Human vs. AI" classifications, this framework evaluates editorial texts across five granular stages of machine intervention:

1. **Stage 1: Completely AI-Generated** – Unlabeled synthetic text generated entirely from prompts without foundational human reporting.
2. **Stage 2: Human-Reported, Heavy AI Prose Reconstruction** – Human reporting notes completely rewritten by an LLM, leaving heavy syntactic machine traces.
3. **Stage 3: Human-Authored, Light AI Structural Proofreading** – Human-written text where AI is restricted to correcting typos, grammar, or passive voice.
4. **Stage 4: Human-Authored, AI-Enriched Vocabulary/Idioms** – Active human-machine collaboration using LLMs as a cross-lingual bridge or thesaurus under strict editorial control.
5. **Stage 5: Organic Human Construction** – Text written completely without generative assistance, retaining natural stylistic entropy and localized etymological markers.
